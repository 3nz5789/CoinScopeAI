## Why MARKET matters specifically for CoinScopeAI

The crypto-trading-tools space is a wash of overlapping categories, each with different trust contracts and different conversion patterns:

- **AI trading bots / "auto-trade for you"** — high install volume, low trust, high churn, regulatory shadow.
- **Signal services** — Telegram-native, gambling-adjacent, low pricing power, frequent rebrands.
- **Trading terminals / charting** — TradingView orbit; high feature bar, mostly free.
- **Risk-management tooling** — small but loyal; bought by the kind of trader who survives.
- **Quant / institutional research platforms** — Kaiko/Amberdata/Numerai-orbit; high contract value, slow sales.
- **Trader OS / journals** — Edgewonk-orbit; small ARPU, sticky.

The category we *say* we're in determines:
- the trust signals we have to carry (a "bot" is judged on copy-trading P&L, a "risk platform" is judged on drawdown discipline);
- the comparison set in the buyer's head;
- which claims we can make legally and which become misleading;
- how much we can charge before friction kicks in.

Picking wrong is a 6-month tax on every other workstream.

- ASSUMPTION — *individual professional traders* and *small crypto funds* (the v1 target list) actually shop in the same category. They may not. (Trader buys a tool; fund buys a platform.) → REQUIRED INPUT from P0 cohort interviews.

- ASSUMPTION — buyers will accept "institutional-grade" without a Series-A or audited track record if backed by published criteria (PCC v2). → DECISION NEEDED before any "institutional-grade" claim ships.

## Failure modes to avoid

- **Category-stacking** — "the AI-powered institutional-grade trader OS" is four claims, none of which are testable. Pick one.
- **Following the loudest competitor** — most loud crypto-AI brands carry liabilities. Don't inherit them by occupying the same shelf.
- **Renaming the category to ourselves** — "we're a *quant cockpit*" is a positioning move, not a category. The buyer searches in their existing vocabulary, not ours.
- **Letting the engine pick the category** — the engine *can* automate trades; that doesn't mean we sell automation. Capability ≠ category.
